For a long time, our lives barely touched. And then somehow, years later, the universe decided we should find our way back into each other's lives.

What happened after that was something I never expected. Because somewhere along the way, you stopped being an old friend and became someone whose victories excited me, whose failures frustrated me, whose tears affected me, and whose happiness genuinely mattered to me.

And if there is one thing that has consistently frustrated me about you, it is not your failures. It's the fact that you still don't see yourself the way I do.

You have spent years underestimating yourself. Years negotiating against your own potential. Years talking yourself out of opportunities before life even had the chance to challenge you.

And honestly, it irritates me. Not because I think little of you. Because I think so much of you.

There were times I snapped. Times I lost patience. Times I became blunt and probably hurt you.

For those moments, I am genuinely sorry.

But I also want you to understand where that came from. Indifference doesn't get frustrated. People don't spend hours arguing with people they don't care about.

Your willingness to be real is one of the things I admire most. Not perfect. Not impressive. Just real.

Over the years, you've shared your dreams, fears, insecurities, frustrations, hopes, disappointments, and pieces of yourself that most people keep hidden.

And I hope you know that I always saw that as trust. A rare kind of trust.

You never needed to earn the right to be yourself around me. You already had it.

You never had to be strong all the time. You never had to pretend. You never had to edit yourself.

Build a life that makes you proud. Build confidence that belongs to you. Build happiness that doesn't depend on anyone else's approval.

I only know that over the years you became one of those rare people whose presence leaves a permanent mark.

The real you.

I am grateful for you.

Not once.
