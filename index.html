If you're reading this, then this was always meant for you.

Some things take years to write.

There are things I should have said years ago. When we reconnected, I never imagined our friendship would become what it is today.

It's just a collection of thoughts that have lived quietly in my mind for years and somehow never found the right words.

When I think about us, I still find it strange how life works. Back in graduation, we were hardly part of each other's lives. We were just two people walking through the same phase, carrying different dreams, different struggles, and heading toward different destinations.

Then life happened. You went through things that would have broken a lot of people. I moved cities, built a career, got married, and followed my own path.

For a long time our lives barely touched. And then somehow, years later, the universe decided we should find our way back into each other's lives.

What happened after that was something I never expected. Because somewhere along the way, you stopped being an old friend and became someone whose victories excited me, whose failures frustrated me, whose tears affected me, and whose happiness genuinely mattered to me.

I've seen you dream. I've seen you break. I've seen you start over. I've seen you doubt yourself. I've seen you aim unbelievably high and then convince yourself you're not capable enough.

And if there is one thing that has consistently frustrated me about you, it is not your failures. It's the fact that you still don't see yourself the way I do.

You have spent years underestimating yourself. Years negotiating against your own potential. Years talking yourself out of opportunities before life even had the chance to challenge you.

And honestly, it irritates me. Not because I think little of you. Because I think so much of you.

There were times I snapped. Times I lost patience. Times I became blunt and probably hurt you.

For those moments, I am genuinely sorry.

But I also want you to understand where that came from. People don't spend hours arguing with people they don't care about. My frustration was never born from judgment. It came from believing in you and wanting more for you than you were willing to demand from yourself.

One of the things I have always admired about you is your willingness to be real. Not perfect. Not polished. Just real.

Over the years you've shared dreams, fears, insecurities, disappointments, ridiculous ideas, ambitions and pieces of yourself that most people never get to see.

And I hope you know that I never saw that openness as a burden. I saw it for what it was. Trust.

You never had to earn the right to be yourself with me. You already had it.

If there is one thing I want for you moving forward, it is this: stop looking at yourself through the eyes of people who cannot fully see your value.

Build something of your own. Build confidence that belongs to you. Build a life that makes you proud. Build happiness that feels genuine when nobody else is watching.

And as for us... I don't really know what label fits our friendship anymore. Maybe none of them do.

I only know that over the years you became one of those rare people whose presence leaves a permanent mark.

You've made me laugh. You've annoyed me. You've tested my patience. You've made me worry. You've made me proud.

And through all of it you've given me the privilege of knowing the person hidden behind expectations, responsibilities, insecurities, dreams and doubts.

The real you.

I am grateful for you.

For every conversation. Every argument. Every ridiculous idea. Every moment of trust. Every year of friendship.

And even on the days when you struggle to believe in yourself, I hope you remember that somewhere in this world there is an idiot who never stopped believing in you.

Not once.
