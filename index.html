For Someone Important

"If you're reading this, then this was always meant for you."

There are things I should have said years ago. When we reconnected, I never imagined our friendship would become what it is today. ... ... ...

I don't really know what to call this. It's not an apology. It's not advice. And it's certainly not a goodbye. It's just a collection of thoughts that have lived in my mind for years and somehow never found the right words. When I think about us, I still find it strange how life works. Back in graduation, we were hardly part of each other's lives. We were just two people walking through the same phase, carrying different dreams, different struggles, and heading toward different destinations. Then life happened. You went through things that would have broken a lot of people. Relationships, disappointments, expectations, responsibilities, compromises. I moved cities, built a career, got married, and followed my own path. For a long time, our lives barely touched. And then somehow, years later, the universe decided we should find our way back into each other's lives. What happened after that was something I never expected. Because somewhere along the way, you stopped being an old friend and became someone whose victories excited me, whose failures frustrated me, whose tears affected me, and whose happiness genuinely mattered to me. I've seen you dream. I've seen you break. I've seen you start over. I've seen you doubt yourself. I've seen you aim unbelievably high and then, in the very next moment, convince yourself you're not capable enough. And if there is one thing that has consistently frustrated me about you, it is not your failures. It's the fact that you still don't see yourself the way I do. You have spent years underestimating yourself. Years negotiating against your own potential. Years talking yourself out of opportunities before life even had the chance to challenge you. And honestly, it irritates me. Not because I think little of you. Because I think so much of you. There were times I snapped. Times I lost patience. Times I raised my voice, became blunt, and probably hurt you. For those moments, I am genuinely sorry. But I also want you to understand where that came from. Indifference doesn't get frustrated. People don't spend hours arguing with people they don't care about. My frustration was never born from judgment. It came from watching somebody I deeply believed in repeatedly settle for less than she deserved from herself. If I pushed, it was because I saw more. If I argued, it was because I cared. If I got angry, it was because I couldn't understand why someone with so much potential continued to doubt her own worth. And despite all of that, despite every disagreement, every lecture, every irritated conversation, you kept coming back. That means more to me than you probably realize. One of the things I have always appreciated about you is your willingness to be real. Not perfect. Not impressive. Just real. Over the years, you've shared your dreams, fears, insecurities, frustrations, hopes, disappointments, random thoughts, ridiculous ideas, and pieces of yourself that most people keep hidden. And I hope you know that I never saw that openness as a burden. I saw it as trust. A rare kind of trust. The kind that can't be demanded, forced, or manufactured. The kind that can only exist when someone feels safe enough to stop pretending. I hope you've always known that you never needed to filter yourself with me. You never needed to show me only the strong version of yourself. You never needed to hide the confused version. Or the scared version. Or the emotional version. Or the ambitious version. Or the version that simply wanted someone to listen without trying to fix everything. You never had to earn the right to be yourself around me. You already had it. And if there is one thing I would want for you moving forward, it is this: Stop looking at yourself through the eyes of people who cannot see your value. Stop measuring your worth through somebody else's effort, attention, approval, or validation. Whether it is family, society, friends, or anyone else. You owe yourself more than that. I want you to build something that belongs to you. A life that makes you proud. A confidence that does not depend on who is standing beside you. A sense of self that cannot be taken away when other people fail to show up. Because you deserve that. You deserve happiness. Real happiness. Not the version that looks acceptable from the outside. The version that feels right within your own heart. And as for us... I don't know what label fits our friendship anymore. Maybe none of them do. I only know that over the years you've become one of those rare people whose presence leaves a permanent mark. You've made me laugh. You've annoyed me. You've tested my patience. You've made me proud. You've made me worry. You've made me think. And through all of it, you've given me the privilege of knowing the person hidden behind the expectations, responsibilities, insecurities, dreams, and doubts. The real you. And that person has always been enough. More than enough. So if there is one thing I want you to remember after reading all of this, it is simple: I am grateful for you. I am grateful for every conversation. Every argument. Every ridiculous idea. Every moment of vulnerability. Every piece of trust. Every year of friendship. And even on the days when you struggle to believe in yourself, I hope you remember that somewhere in this world there is an idiot who never stopped believing in you. Not once. --- **- Your friend**
